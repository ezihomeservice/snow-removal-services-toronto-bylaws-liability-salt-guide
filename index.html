Snow Removal Services Toronto: A Homeowner’s Guide to Bylaws, Liability, and Smart Salting

Toronto winters do not follow a schedule. Some years bring a few light dustings. Other years bring a storm that buries the whole city in a day. Either way, the snow on your driveway and walkway is your job to deal with. This guide explains the rules, the legal risks, and how to pick snow removal services in Toronto that keep you safe without wasting money or salt.

The short version:

Clear your sidewalk within 12 hours when snowfall is under 2 cm. Clear private areas like driveways and steps quickly too. Use salt sparingly, and book a service by the visit if you want flexibility. Never push snow onto the road or sidewalk.

Why Winter Planning Matters in Toronto

Think of a snowstorm like a surprise guest. You cannot control when it shows up, but you can decide whether you are ready.

Toronto’s average winter snowfall is about 121.5 cm at the downtown weather station and 108.5 cm at Pearson Airport. Averages hide big swings, though. By the first day of spring in 2026, 189.2 cm had fallen at Pearson, about 75 cm more than a typical winter, according to a retired Environment Canada climatologist. A late-January storm that year brought what Environment Canada called 61 cm to downtown Toronto in a day.

Because the totals can swing so much, many households prefer to pay for clearing only when it snows, instead of committing to a full season up front.

What Toronto’s Rules Say About Clearing Snow

The City of Toronto explains its rules on its page on clearing snow and ice from your property. In short:

SituationWhat the City says
Snowfall under 2 cmResidents and businesses clear the sidewalk next to their property of snow and ice within 12 hours of the end of the snowfall.
Snowfall of 2 cm or moreCity crews clear public sidewalks. This can take about 12 hours and can continue up to 72 hours after snow stops.
Pushing snow off your propertyNot allowed onto sidewalks, roads, or bike lanes. The City lists a $500 fine plus a $110 victim surcharge.
Fire hydrants and catch basinsResidents are asked to clear snow around hydrants and, where safe, the storm grates near their home.

Private property has its own timing. As Narcity reports, the Property Standards Bylaw expects owners to clear driveways, parking spaces, steps, ramps, and landings within 24 hours of the end of the snowfall. Fine amounts have changed over the years and are quoted differently across news stories, so check the City’s page for the current figures. You can also report a property that is not cleared by calling 311.

One more rule matters for anyone who hires help. A lawyer quoted by Legal Matters notes that Ontario’s Highway Traffic Act makes it illegal to shovel snow and ice onto the road, and that includes contractors. Ask any crew where they plan to put the snow.

Who Is Responsible if Someone Slips on Your Walkway?

Under Ontario’s Occupiers’ Liability Act, people who control a property must take reasonable care to keep visitors reasonably safe. A legal summary from Mondaq on the Court of Appeal’s Cannon v. Cemcor decision stresses that occupiers are not insurers, and that perfection is not required. A reasonable winter plan is what counts.

What does reasonable look like? In one case discussed by Mondaq, a property manager and snow contractor were found not to have met their duty because salt was not applied in a timely way. The same case also found the person who fell partly responsible for not taking extra care in winter conditions.

There is also a short deadline. Since 2021, a person hurt by snow or ice generally must give written notice of the claim within 60 days to an occupier or to the snow removal contractor. That notice must reach at least one of them, and the law applies to the contractor too. For you, the lesson is simple: keep records of when you shovelled and salted, and keep your contractor’s invoices.

This is general information, not legal advice. If someone is hurt on your property, talk to your insurer and a lawyer.

How to Use Salt the Smart Way

Salt helps, but too much of it harms lawns, pipes, and waterways. Ontario municipalities alone use more than 100,000 tonnes of road salt every year, and homeowners add to it. Here is what the guidance says:

  • Shovel first. Remove snow and ice before you salt, so the salt has less work to do (Water Rangers).
  • Use less than you think. One coffee mug of salt is about enough for a single-car driveway or 10 sidewalk squares, according to the same guidance.
  • Watch the temperature. Salt works less well below –10 °C and may not work at all below about –21 °C, according to CBC. Sand adds grip in extreme cold, although it does not melt ice.
  • Time it. Salting before a storm can help, but it is much less effective during heavy rain.

If you hire a crew, a town environment page in Ontario suggests choosing a contractor certified in Smart About Salt, and paying by snowfall event or by season rather than by how much salt is used. That way no one has a reason to over-salt.

Seasonal Contract or Pay Per Visit?

  • Pay per visit: Good when snowfall is unpredictable, when you are away often, or when you can handle light snow yourself.
  • Seasonal contract: Good if you want guaranteed service for every storm and a fixed budget. Read what counts as a “storm” and what is excluded.
  • Do it yourself: Fine for a short driveway if you are healthy and have time. Heavy, wet snow is hard on the heart and back, so be honest about your limits.

Questions to Ask Before You Book

  • How soon after a snowfall do you arrive, and is there a guaranteed window?
  • Does the price include the walkway, steps, and windrow?
  • How and where do you pile the snow? Do you keep it off the road and sidewalk?
  • How much salt do you use, and do you follow salt-saving practices?
  • Do you carry liability insurance, and can you send proof?
  • Will I get a record of each visit, such as a time stamp or a photo?
  • What is the cost to reschedule or cancel?

Conclusion

The best snow removal services in Toronto are the ones that match the rules and the weather. Know your 12-hour sidewalk duty, keep snow on your own property, and keep records in case of a claim. Use salt in small amounts, and choose a service that is clear about price, timing, and where the snow goes. Do that before the first storm, and winter gets a lot less stressful.

Frequently Asked Questions

How soon do I have to clear my sidewalk in Toronto?

When snowfall is under 2 cm, the City says residents and businesses must clear the sidewalk next to their property within 12 hours of the end of the snowfall. For 2 cm or more, City crews clear public sidewalks.

Can I push snow onto the street?

No. The City says pushing snow onto sidewalks, roads, or bike lanes is dangerous and illegal, and lists a $500 fine plus a $110 surcharge. This applies to hired crews as well.

What if someone slips on my walkway?

Ontario law expects occupiers to take reasonable care. Injured people generally have to give written notice within 60 days to an occupier or snow contractor. Contact your insurer and a lawyer for advice.

How much salt should I use?

Less than you think. About one coffee mug covers a single-car driveway or 10 sidewalk squares. Shovel first, and avoid salting in extreme cold, when it works poorly.

How much does snow removal cost in Toronto?

It depends on the company and the job. Ezi lists $81 for a single driveway and $96 for a double driveway per visit, with add-ons such as windrow clearing for $40. Always compare written quotes.

Is a seasonal contract better than booking per visit?

It depends. A contract gives you guaranteed service, while per-visit booking suits unpredictable winters and flexible budgets. Read what the contract counts as a storm.
